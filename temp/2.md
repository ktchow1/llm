# Risk Model for the ILP Algorithm

This note describes the covariance and portfolio-risk model used by the ILP algorithm. It covers covariance reconstruction, conversion between tradable and outright positions, the liquidity-adjusted risk objective, single-security risk reduction, implied tradable positions, implementation outputs, and the connection to LASSO.

## 1. Security domains

The model uses two sets of securities:

- **Outright set:** `M` securities used for covariance calibration.
- **Tradable set:** `N` securities, where `N >= M`. This set includes the outrights and may also include spreads, butterflies, packs, or other strategies.

For the EGB example, the outright set contains seven near and seven far futures, so `M = 14`:

- FGBS near and far.
- FGBM near and far.
- FGBL near and far.
- FGBX near and far.
- FBTS near and far.
- FBTP near and far.
- FOAT near and far.

## 2. Covariance reconstruction in `FCovar`

An overnight `qfilib` job calculates the covariance matrix of daily returns for the outright set. It performs rank-3 PCA and stores:

- The first three eigenvectors.
- Their three eigenvalues.
- One residual variance for each outright.

The result is stored in KDB, published through `egb_refdata`, and consumed by the ILP algorithm. The three principal components approximately represent curve level, tilt, and curvature.

`FCovar` reconstructs the outright covariance matrix as:

$$
C_{\mathrm{out}}=Q\Lambda Q^T+D,
\qquad
Q\in\mathbb R^{M\times3},
\quad
\Lambda\in\mathbb R^{3\times3},
\quad
D\in\mathbb R^{M\times M}.
\tag{1}
$$

Here, `D` is diagonal. Its residual variances restore the diagonal variances lost by the rank-3 approximation.

Covariance calibration depends only on the outright set. Algorithms with different tradable sets but the same outrights can therefore share one `FCovar` instance without recalibration.

## 3. Tradable and outright representations

Use the following vectors:

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_{\mathrm{trd}}&\in\mathbb R^N
&&\text{tradable positions},\\
X_{\mathrm{out}}&\in\mathbb R^M
&&\text{equivalent outright positions},\\
P_{\mathrm{trd}}&\in\mathbb R^N
&&\text{tradable prices},\\
P_{\mathrm{out}}&\in\mathbb R^M
&&\text{outright prices}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Let `S` be the `N x M` replication matrix. Each row describes one tradable in outright units. For example, a spread row contains `+1, -1`; a butterfly row contains `-1, +2, -1`.

No-arbitrage pricing, equal portfolio value, and position decomposition are:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathrm{trd}}&=SP_{\mathrm{out}},\\
X_{\mathrm{trd}}^TP_{\mathrm{trd}}
  &=X_{\mathrm{out}}^TP_{\mathrm{out}},\\
X_{\mathrm{out}}&=S^TX_{\mathrm{trd}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

The framework code may store the transpose of the `S` convention used here.

## 4. Covariance and portfolio variance

Because tradable price changes replicate outright price changes:

$$
\Delta P_{\mathrm{trd}}=S\Delta P_{\mathrm{out}}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
C_{\mathrm{trd}}
=\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta P_{\mathrm{trd}})
=SC_{\mathrm{out}}S^T.
\tag{3}
$$

This calculation is implemented in `FRisk::updateCovar`.

Portfolio variance can be evaluated in either domain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{risk}
&=X_{\mathrm{trd}}^TC_{\mathrm{trd}}X_{\mathrm{trd}}\\
&=X_{\mathrm{trd}}^TSC_{\mathrm{out}}S^TX_{\mathrm{trd}}\\
&=X_{\mathrm{out}}^TC_{\mathrm{out}}X_{\mathrm{out}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

The implementation uses the last expression in `FRisk::updateRisk`. Only the outright covariance needs overnight calibration; no tradable covariance needs to be stored in KDB.

## 5. Liquidity-adjusted risk objective

Let `lq` be the nonnegative liquidity-cost vector. The cost is proportional to absolute position, so long and short positions of the same magnitude have equal cost.

The `FRisk` objective is:

$$
f(X_{\mathrm{trd}})
=X_{\mathrm{out}}^TC_{\mathrm{out}}X_{\mathrm{out}}
+2\,lq^T|X_{\mathrm{trd}}|
=X_{\mathrm{trd}}^TC_{\mathrm{trd}}X_{\mathrm{trd}}
+2\,lq^T|X_{\mathrm{trd}}|.
\tag{5}
$$

Absolute value is elementwise. Away from zero positions, define the sign vector:

$$
\mu_n=\operatorname{sign}(x_{\mathrm{trd},n}).
$$

At zero, the subgradient is the interval from minus one to plus one. The half-gradient used by the framework is:

$$
\frac12\nabla f(X_{\mathrm{trd}})
=SC_{\mathrm{out}}X_{\mathrm{out}}+lq\odot\mu.
\tag{6}
$$

Here, the multiplication symbol denotes elementwise multiplication.

| Code item | Mathematical quantity |
|---|---|
| `FRisk::value()` | Liquidity-adjusted objective in Equation 5 |
| `FRisk::m_bias` | Half-gradient in Equation 6 |

## 6. Marginal risk reduction for one tradable

A simultaneous perfect hedge would set all positions to zero, but this assumes unlimited synchronized liquidity and zero latency. In practice, the algorithm considers one tradable at a time.

For tradable `k`, change only its position:

$$
X_{\mathrm{trd}}^{\mathrm{new}}
=X_{\mathrm{trd}}+\Delta x\,e_k,
\tag{7}
$$

where `e_k` is the unit vector for coordinate `k`. Define:

$$
g_k=S_kC_{\mathrm{out}}X_{\mathrm{out}},
\qquad
q_k=C_{\mathrm{trd},k,k},
\qquad
x_k=x_{\mathrm{trd},k}.
$$

After discarding terms independent of the proposed trade, the one-dimensional objective is:

$$
f_k(\Delta x)
=q_k(\Delta x)^2+2g_k\Delta x
+2\,lq_k|x_k+\Delta x|+\mathrm{const}.
\tag{8}
$$

Let the new position be `y = x_k + Delta x`. Completing the square gives:

$$
f_k(y)
=q_k\left(y-z_k\right)^2+2\,lq_k|y|+\mathrm{const},
\qquad
z_k=x_k-\frac{g_k}{q_k}.
\tag{9}
$$

Use the soft-threshold operator:

$$
\mathcal S(z,\gamma)
=\operatorname{sign}(z)\max(|z|-\gamma,0).
$$

The optimal new position and trade are therefore:

$$
\boxed{
y_k^*=\mathcal S\left(z_k,\frac{lq_k}{q_k}\right),
\qquad
\Delta x_k^*=y_k^*-x_k.
}
\tag{10}
$$

Equivalently:

$$
\Delta x_k^*=
\begin{cases}
-\dfrac{g_k+lq_k}{q_k},
& z_k>\dfrac{lq_k}{q_k},\\[6pt]
-\dfrac{g_k-lq_k}{q_k},
& z_k<-\dfrac{lq_k}{q_k},\\[6pt]
-x_k,
& |z_k|\le\dfrac{lq_k}{q_k}.
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
$$

The last case closes the entire position. It applies when the marginal variance-reduction benefit does not exceed the liquidity cost. The dead zone is a region for the unpenalized target `z_k`, not for the trade itself.

| Code item | Mathematical quantity |
|---|---|
| `FRisk::m_targetVar[k]` | `q_k`, variance of tradable `k` |
| `FRisk::m_maxRiskRed[k]` | `Delta x_k*`, optimal single-security trade |

## 7. Implied tradable position

The mapping from tradable to outright positions is generally many-to-one:

$$
X_{\mathrm{out}}=S^TX_{\mathrm{trd}},
\qquad N\ge M.
$$

Consequently, an outright position does not uniquely identify a tradable position. A cleaner interface would supply `X_trd` to `FRisk` and derive `X_out` internally. The current interface receives `X_out`, so it must estimate tradable positions.

### 7.1 Least-squares estimate

If the system has the required rank and dimensions, a least-squares estimate solves:

$$
\widehat X_{\mathrm{trd}}
=\arg\min_X\|S^TX-X_{\mathrm{out}}\|_2^2.
\tag{12}
$$

Under the source document's full-row-rank assumption:

$$
\widehat X_{\mathrm{trd}}
=(SS^T)^{-1}SX_{\mathrm{out}}.
\tag{13}
$$

`FRisk` does not use this solution.

### 7.2 Current projection estimate

The current implementation projects the outright book separately onto every tradable row:

$$
\widehat x_{\mathrm{trd},n}
=\frac{S_nX_{\mathrm{out}}}{\|S_n\|_2^2}.
\tag{14}
$$

This answers the hypothetical question: if the entire outright book were represented only by tradable `n`, how many units would best approximate it?

For the outright position and three spread rows:

$$
X_{\mathrm{out}}=
\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-1\end{bmatrix}^T,
\quad
S_0=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\end{bmatrix},
\quad
S_1=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&-1\end{bmatrix},
\quad
S_2=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-1\end{bmatrix},
$$

the independent projections are:

$$
\widehat X_{\mathrm{trd}}
=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac12&\tfrac12&1\end{bmatrix}^T.
$$

These values are separate per-tradable projections, not one jointly consistent decomposition.

## 8. `FRisk` output summary

| Code item | Definition |
|---|---|
| `value()` | Liquidity-adjusted risk objective |
| `m_bias` | Half-gradient in tradable space |
| `m_maxRiskRed[n]` | Optimal trade when only tradable `n` may change |
| `m_impliedPos[n]` | Projection of the outright book onto tradable `n` |
| `m_targetVar[n]` | Variance of tradable `n` |
| `m_targetVol[n]` | Volatility of tradable `n` |
| `m_benchRisk` | Variance of the configured benchmark amount |
| `m_benchQty[n]` | Volatility-equivalent quantity of tradable `n` |
| `m_targetRange[n]` | Quantity producing the configured benchmark-risk multiple |

The corresponding formulas are:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\texttt{m\_impliedPos}[n]
  &=\frac{S_nX_{\mathrm{out}}}{\|S_n\|_2^2},\\
\texttt{m\_targetVar}[n]
  &=C_{\mathrm{trd},n,n},\\
\texttt{m\_targetVol}[n]
  &=\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{trd},n,n}},\\
\texttt{m\_benchRisk}
  &=A_{\mathrm{bench}}^2C_{\mathrm{trd},b,b},\\
\texttt{m\_benchQty}[n]
  &=A_{\mathrm{bench}}
    \sqrt{\frac{C_{\mathrm{trd},b,b}}{C_{\mathrm{trd},n,n}}},\\
\texttt{m\_targetRange}[n]
  &=\sqrt{
    \frac{
      R_{\mathrm{bench}}A_{\mathrm{bench}}^2C_{\mathrm{trd},b,b}
    }{
      C_{\mathrm{trd},n,n}
    }}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$

Here, `b` is the benchmark-security index, `A_bench` is the benchmark amount, and `R_bench` is the configured risk multiple.

## 9. Appendix on LASSO and coordinate descent

For a least-squares problem, the L1- and L2-regularized objectives are:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_X\;&\|AX-B\|_2^2+\lambda\|X\|_1
&&\text{LASSO},\\
\min_X\;&\|AX-B\|_2^2+\lambda\|X\|_2^2
&&\text{ridge regression}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$

L2 regularization has the closed-form solution:

$$
X^*=(A^TA+\lambda I)^{-1}A^TB.
\tag{17}
$$

In production numerical code, solving the linear system is generally preferable to explicitly computing the inverse.

L1 regularization is not differentiable at zero. Enumerating every sign combination requires up to three possibilities per coordinate and is infeasible in high dimensions. Coordinate descent avoids this by optimizing one coordinate at a time.

Geometrically, L2 penalty contours are circular or spherical, whereas L1 contours have corners on the coordinate axes. Those corners make exact zero-valued coordinates likely and explain LASSO's sparsity.

Let:

$$
r=AX-B,
\qquad
A_k=Ae_k,
\qquad
C_{k,k}=A_k^TA_k.
$$

While holding every other coordinate fixed, replace `x_k` by `y`. The coordinate objective reduces to:

$$
f_k(y)
=C_{k,k}(y-z_k)^2+\lambda|y|+\mathrm{const},
\qquad
z_k=x_k-\frac{A_k^Tr}{C_{k,k}}.
\tag{18}
$$

The coordinate-descent update is:

$$
\boxed{
x_k^{\mathrm{new}}
=\mathcal S\left(
x_k-\frac{A_k^Tr}{C_{k,k}},
\frac{\lambda}{2C_{k,k}}
\right).
}
\tag{19}
$$

The corresponding move is the new value minus the old value. If the unpenalized coordinate target lies in the threshold interval, the new coordinate is exactly zero. This is why LASSO produces sparse parameter vectors and performs feature selection.

By contrast, a support-vector machine produces sparsity over data points through its support vectors; it performs data selection rather than feature selection.

## 10. Compact formula summary

The core model can be transferred to Confluence using three LaTeX boxes.

Covariance and domain conversion:

$$
\begin{gathered}
C_{\mathrm{out}}=Q\Lambda Q^T+D,
\qquad
P_{\mathrm{trd}}=SP_{\mathrm{out}},
\qquad
X_{\mathrm{out}}=S^TX_{\mathrm{trd}},\\
C_{\mathrm{trd}}=SC_{\mathrm{out}}S^T,
\qquad
\operatorname{risk}
=X_{\mathrm{out}}^TC_{\mathrm{out}}X_{\mathrm{out}}.
\end{gathered}
$$

Liquidity-adjusted objective and gradient:

$$
\begin{gathered}
f(X_{\mathrm{trd}})
=X_{\mathrm{out}}^TC_{\mathrm{out}}X_{\mathrm{out}}
+2\,lq^T|X_{\mathrm{trd}}|,\\
\frac12\nabla f
=SC_{\mathrm{out}}X_{\mathrm{out}}+lq\odot\operatorname{sign}(X_{\mathrm{trd}}).
\end{gathered}
$$

Optimal one-security hedge:

$$
\begin{gathered}
g_k=S_kC_{\mathrm{out}}X_{\mathrm{out}},
\qquad
q_k=C_{\mathrm{trd},k,k},
\qquad
z_k=x_k-\frac{g_k}{q_k},\\
y_k^*=\mathcal S\left(z_k,\frac{lq_k}{q_k}\right),
\qquad
\Delta x_k^*=y_k^*-x_k.
\end{gathered}
$$
